# Exit Rules, Stop-Losses and Market-Timing Overlays for Long-Only Momentum / Swing Trading (Taiwan focus, as of Nov 2026)

Reading conventions used throughout:
- **[R+]** = evidence that the rule raises return or Sharpe. **[DD-only]** = reduces drawdown/volatility but at equal or lower return. **[Noise]** = no reliable effect. **[Contested]** = failed replication or serious critique exists.
- "OOS" = out-of-sample. Unless marked OOS, results are in-sample backtests.
- Verification level: most numbers below come from abstracts or reputable secondary summaries (CXO Advisory, Alpha Architect, RePEc abstracts), not from the papers' tables. Where I could not verify a number against a source it is listed under Gaps, not Findings.
- One Taiwan source (`nfuacademic.nfu.edu.tw`) could not be fetched: the host is outside this session's allowed network domains. Only its search snippet is used.

## 1. Stop-loss rules: what does the evidence say?

### Takeaway
Stop-losses are not a free lunch: under a random walk they strictly lower expected return, and they only add value when returns are positively autocorrelated (trending) or regime-switching. On individual stocks, tight stops (a few % over 1 day) lose to buy-and-hold after costs, wide stops (about 10–15% over multi-day windows) are roughly return-neutral with modestly lower downside, and the one paper showing a large return gain (Han, Zhou & Zhu) is gross of costs, on a long-short US portfolio, and flagged for data-snooping. Classification: wide stop = **[DD-only]** by default, **[R+]** only for genuinely momentum-like holdings; tight stop = negative.

### Cited Findings

**Kaminski & Lo (working paper 2008; Journal of Financial Markets 2014, vol. 18, 234–254)**
- Theory: if the asset follows a random walk, the "stopping premium" (return of stop-loss strategy minus buy-and-hold) is always negative; under momentum / positive serial correlation the stopping premium can be positive. — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876)
- Empirics are on a buy-and-hold US index futures strategy with long-term bond futures as the safe asset, not on individual stocks. At longer sampling frequencies certain stop policies raised expected return while substantially reducing volatility. — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876)
- A secondary summary reports the volatility-based stop rules added up to about 1.5% per month in the 1993–2011 sample with bonds as the safe harbour. This is a secondary figure and the gain depends on the bond bull market of that period. — [Seeking Alpha summary](https://seekingalpha.com/article/3257295-momentum-and-stop-losses)

**Lo & Remorov (Nov 2015 working paper; Journal of Financial Markets 2017, vol. 34, 1–15)** — the most directly relevant study for individual stocks
- Sample: daily returns, broad sample of US common stocks, 1964–2014; simulations use 0.2% cost per trade (average half-spread 2013–14); empirical tests use actual or estimated half-spreads. In-sample across many settings. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-premium/stop-losses-on-stock-positions-in-depth)
- Stops tested: cumulative return over 1 day falling below 0% to −6% ("tight"), and over 10 days falling below 0% to −14% ("wide"); on trigger, switch to T-bills; re-enter when cumulative return over a set window exceeds a threshold. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-premium/stop-losses-on-stock-positions-in-depth)
- Result: tight stops significantly lower returns, mainly from trading costs. Wide stops have lower raw returns than buy-and-hold, and on a risk-adjusted basis the wide 10-day stops perform about as well as buy-and-hold. Downside-risk reduction exists but is "not substantial". — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-premium/stop-losses-on-stock-positions-in-depth); [MIT DSpace abstract](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/107017)
- Condition for outperformance: sufficiently high serial correlation in the stock's returns. US stock autocorrelations were positive in the first half of the sample and significantly negative in the second half, which is why tight stops are "not well suited to recent US stock behaviour". — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-premium/stop-losses-on-stock-positions-in-depth)
- Regime-switching simulations: wide stops beat buy-and-hold when volatility is low in bull regimes, high in bear regimes, and switches are infrequent; the 10-day signal raises skewness and cuts max drawdown. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-premium/stop-losses-on-stock-positions-in-depth)
- Execution: delayed execution (signal at close, sell at next day's close) is marginally better than same-close execution for most settings and significantly better for tight stops, because of one-day return reversal. Quick re-entry is usually beneficial for tight stops. Large bid-ask spreads commonly accompany crashes, working against stops. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-premium/stop-losses-on-stock-positions-in-depth)

**Han, Zhou & Zhu, "Taming Momentum Crashes: A Simple Stop-Loss Strategy" (SSRN working paper, 2014–2016)** — **[R+] but [Contested]**
- Sample: US common stocks, Jan 1926–Dec 2013; 6-month formation, 1-month skip, monthly holding; long-short winners minus losers, equal-weighted. In-sample. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/stop-losses-to-avoid-stock-momentum-crashes/)
- Stops tested: 10%, 15%, 20% from the start-of-month price, checked daily; proceeds sit in T-bills until month end, then the portfolio is re-formed (so re-entry is by time, at the next monthly rebalance). — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/stop-losses-to-avoid-stock-momentum-crashes/)
- 15% stop vs plain momentum (monthly, gross): mean return 1.93% vs 0.99%; standard deviation 4.61% vs 6.01%; Sharpe 0.40 vs 0.17; worst month −17.4% vs −49.8%. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/stop-losses-to-avoid-stock-momentum-crashes/)
- 10% stop: Sharpe 0.50, worst month −15.4%. 20% stop: Sharpe 0.32, worst month −16.79%. Tighter looked better in this gross test. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/stop-losses-to-avoid-stock-momentum-crashes/)
- Cost of the stop: winner-side monthly turnover rises from 39% to 49%; about 10% of long-side stocks are stopped out each month at the 15% level. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/stop-losses-to-avoid-stock-momentum-crashes/)
- Caveats flagged by CXO: all results gross of costs; frictions are very high for illiquid stocks in stop conditions; possible data-snooping in lookback, threshold and execution rules; results may hinge on a few crash months; individual investors may not hold enough stocks for the result to be reliable. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/stop-losses-to-avoid-stock-momentum-crashes/)
- The paper remains an unpublished working paper, and Alpha Architect published a piece titled "Taming the Momentum Roller Coaster: Fact or Fiction?" examining it. — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2016/08/taming-the-momentum-roller-coaster-fact-or-fiction/)

**Clare, Seaton, Smith & Thomas, "Breaking into the Blackbox" (working paper 2012; Journal of Asset Management 2013, vol. 14, 182–194)** — **[Noise]** for stops
- Scope is the S&P 500 index, not individual stocks. Conclusion: popular stop-loss rules (including trailing stops) do not add value on top of trend-following rules, and end-of-month decision rules beat rules that trade more frequently. — [City Research Online](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/17842)
- Short-term signals give far worse returns than long ones because of over-trading; long-interval trend rules beat buy-and-hold on the S&P 500; monthly calculation beats daily. Samples: Jul 1988–Jun 2011 (daily) and Jan 1952–Jun 2011 (monthly). — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/following-sp-500-index-trends)

**Taiwan evidence**
- A Taiwan study on TAIEX index futures (National Formosa University journal) compared four strategies including fixed-days stop and ATR stop against buy-and-hold: buy-and-hold was significantly better than both the fixed-days stop and the ATR stop, most clearly over periods covering a full bull-bear cycle. Search-snippet level only; futures, not single stocks. — [NFU journal PDF (host not reachable from this session)](https://nfuacademic.nfu.edu.tw/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2025/06/04停損與買入持有之損益比較-以台股指數期貨為例.pdf)
- An NCCU thesis on fund investing found that an allocation-type dollar-cost-averaging plan with simultaneous 10% take-profit and 10% stop-loss had the lowest risk and the best Sharpe and Sortino among the variants tested. This is a DCA fund context, not single-stock momentum. — [NCCU repository](https://nccur.lib.nccu.edu.tw/handle/140.119/95051)

### Inferences
- The theory gives a testable precondition: a stop helps only if "a stock that has just fallen X% tends to keep falling". In a backtest, measure the forward return of stopped-out positions over the remaining intended holding period. If it is not negative net of the extra round-trip cost (0.6–0.9% here), the stop has a negative stopping premium and should be dropped or widened.
- Widths supported by evidence for single stocks: about 10–15% measured over multi-day windows, or from entry/period start. Evidence against: stops tighter than about 5–6% on daily moves. No academic study found tests ATR-multiple stops on single momentum stocks; the one Taiwan ATR test (index futures) was negative.
- Han–Zhou–Zhu's gain comes disproportionately from avoiding a handful of crash months and from the short leg; a long-only Taiwan trader should expect a much smaller benefit, most plausibly a drawdown cap rather than a return gain.
- Lo–Remorov's "delay one day" result fits an end-of-day workflow: decide at close, sell next session. It also means intraday stop orders are not obviously better.
- With a 0.6–0.9% round trip, each false stop followed by re-entry costs roughly 1.2–1.8% of position value (two round trips versus none), so the stop must be wide enough to trigger rarely.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed test of stop-losses on Taiwan individual momentum stocks was found. The Taiwan evidence is on index futures and funds.
- I did not read the content of Alpha Architect's "Fact or Fiction" piece, so I cannot state whether it replicates or refutes Han–Zhou–Zhu; treat the paper as unpublished and unconfirmed.
- No rigorous academic comparison of fixed-% versus ATR-multiple versus trailing (chandelier-type) stops on single stocks was found; these would need to be established in the user's own backtest.
- Exact return/Sharpe/drawdown tables in Lo–Remorov and Kaminski–Lo were not retrieved.

## 2. Time-based exits versus signal-based exits; profit targets and the disposition effect

### Takeaway
The academic momentum literature is built almost entirely on time-based exits (hold N months, re-rank, replace), and the studies that add signal exits find that frequent-decision rules and trailing stops add nothing over slow, periodic decisions on an index. Profit targets work against the mechanism that makes momentum pay: Taiwan individual investors already sell winners about twice as readily as losers, which is the documented losing behaviour, so a fixed profit target formalises the disposition effect. No direct head-to-head test of profit-target versus time exit on momentum stocks was found.

### Cited Findings
- Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean (European Financial Management 2007, 13(3), 423–447), using every trade and trader on the Taiwan Stock Exchange: investors are about twice as likely to sell a stock held at a gain as one held at a loss. Individuals, corporations and dealers show this reluctance to realise losses; mutual funds and foreign investors do not, and those two groups are under 5% of trade value. — [RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/eufman/v13y2007i3p423-447.html)
- Clare et al. (2013), S&P 500 index: popular stop-loss rules do not add value and monthly end-of-month decisions beat more frequent trading. — [City Research Online](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/17842)
- Han, Zhou & Zhu's design is a hybrid: a daily-checked stop inside a fixed one-month holding period, with re-ranking (time-based re-entry) at month end. Its gross Sharpe improvement (0.17 to 0.40 at 15%) is the only evidence found in which a signal exit improves a momentum portfolio. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/stop-losses-to-avoid-stock-momentum-crashes/)
- Taiwan practitioner backtest (2016–2026): entering/exiting on the yearly line (SMA240) and quarterly line (SMA60) returned +1,427% on TSMC versus +2,069% buy-and-hold, and +1,177% on MediaTek versus +3,033%; the rule only helped on cyclical names such as Yageo. Practitioner article, in-sample, single names. — [search results for Taiwan MA backtests; exact article URL not pinned, see Gaps](https://www.moneyweekly.com.tw/_Article?AID=230109)

### Inferences
- A defensible default for a 2-week-to-3-month holder: exit on (a) loss of ranking/eligibility at a periodic review (weekly or monthly), plus (b) a wide catastrophic stop. No fixed profit target.
- A trailing stop is the signal exit most consistent with the evidence because it leaves the right tail open, but there is no clean evidence that it beats time/rank exits; it should be judged in backtest by whether it lowers drawdown without cutting CAGR by more than the drawdown saved is worth.
- Moving-average-break exits on individual winners tend to give back large trend gains in strong names (TSMC/MediaTek example) and help mainly on cyclicals: classify as **[DD-only]**, stock-type dependent.
- Because Taiwan retail flow is disposition-prone, selling pressure from profit-taking after run-ups is a plausible reason winners under-react and keep drifting; a rule set should hold winners longer than feels comfortable and cut losers faster.

### Gaps
- No study was found that directly compares profit-target exits with time-based exits on momentum portfolios, in any market. The claim that targets "cut the right tail" is an inference from the disposition literature and from the positive skew of trend returns, not a measured effect size.
- Odean (1998) figures were not retrieved in this session; from memory (unverified) it reported the proportion of gains realised exceeding the proportion of losses realised, and that winners sold subsequently outperformed losers held.
- The article URL for the TSMC/MediaTek SMA240/SMA60 backtest could not be pinned to one of the returned links (moneyweekly, businessweekly or LINE Today); verify before citing.

## 3. Optimal holding period, rebalancing frequency and banding rules under transaction costs

### Takeaway
After costs, turnover is the deciding variable: Novy-Marx & Velikov find anomalies with under 50% monthly turnover mostly survive when implemented with cost mitigation, few above that do, and the single most effective mitigation is a buy/hold spread (strict rule to enter, looser rule to stay). Whether momentum survives costs at all is contested and depends on who is trading; for a retail trader with a 0.6–0.9% round trip, monthly-to-quarterly review with a hold band is the evidence-backed design, and holding periods of only 2 weeks need a correspondingly larger gross edge.

### Cited Findings
- Novy-Marx & Velikov, "A Taxonomy of Anomalies and Their Trading Costs" (NBER WP 20721, 2014; RFS 2016, 29(1), 104–147), US stocks: a buy/hold spread, "more stringent requirements for establishing positions than for maintaining them", is the most effective cost-mitigation technique. Most anomalies with less than 50% turnover per month generate significant net spreads when designed to mitigate costs; few with higher turnover do. Costs use Hasbrouck's effective bid-ask spread estimator. — [NBER w20721](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20721)
- Same paper: transaction costs always reduce profitability and so increase data-snooping concerns. — [NBER w20721](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20721)
- Frazzini, Israel & Moskowitz (AQR working paper, Dec 2012), using live trading data from a large institutional manager: real trading costs are well below earlier estimates; size, value and momentum survive costs with substantial capacity; short-term reversal does not. — [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/surprise-the-size-value-and-momentum-anomalies-survive-after-trading-costs/)
- **[Contested]** Lesmond, Schill & Zhou (JFE 2004): momentum profits are an "illusion" because the stocks generating the large momentum returns are precisely those with high trading costs. — [Lesmond et al. 2004 PDF](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/rsusmel/phd/Lesmond_et%20al%20_2004_JFE.pdf)
- **[Contested]** Patton & Weller (2018/2020), using mutual-fund-level data: after implementation costs typical mutual funds earn low returns to value and no returns to momentum. — [Patton & Weller PDF](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Patton_Weller_MF_28nov18.pdf)
- Alpha Architect (Vogel, 2015), US 1970s–2010s backtest, gross of costs: a 50-stock, monthly-rebalanced momentum portfolio earned 17.36% CAGR versus 10.89% for a 500-stock annually rebalanced one. A 50-stock quarterly-rebalanced version showed 16.87% gross CAGR, about 14.87% net of an assumed 1% fee and 0.25% per rebalance. Their conclusion: concentrated (about 50 names), monthly-to-quarterly rebalance. — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/how-portfolio-construction-affects-momentum-funds/)
- Han, Zhou & Zhu: plain 6-month momentum has about 39% monthly turnover on the winner side, 49% with a 15% stop. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/stop-losses-to-avoid-stock-momentum-crashes/)
- Taiwan 52-week-high evidence is supportive but mixed: one study calls the 52-week-high strategy's profitability "remarkable" in Taiwan and stronger than 12-month-return momentum; another finds the effect is weakened when the market index is itself near its high. — [Journal of International Financial Markets, Institutions & Money 2011](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfin/v21y2011i1p14-27.html); [IJBESAR Taiwan 52-week-high study](https://ijbesar.ihu.gr/docs/volume16_issue1/16_01_07.pdf)

### Inferences
- Cost arithmetic for the user: at 0.6–0.9% per round trip, a fully turned-over portfolio pays about 7–11% per year at monthly turnover (12 round trips), 2.4–3.6% at quarterly, and 15–23% at bi-weekly (26 round trips). A 2-week holding period therefore needs a gross edge several times larger than a 2–3 month one.
- 39% monthly winner-side turnover (plain momentum) implies an average holding period of roughly 2.5 months, which sits inside the user's 2-week-to-3-month window and under the 50% threshold; adding a stop pushes it to the threshold.
- A banding rule such as "buy only in the top 10% of the ranking, hold until it leaves the top 30%" is the direct retail translation of the buy/hold spread. The specific 10%/30% widths are illustrative; the paper supports the mechanism, and the widths should be tuned in backtest against turnover.
- Review cadence: weekly review with a hold band approximates monthly effective turnover; daily re-ranking without a band is the configuration the evidence most clearly penalises.

### Gaps
- Novy-Marx & Velikov's momentum-specific numbers (gross vs net monthly spread, turnover, the exact band they use) were not retrieved; check the NBER PDF tables.
- No Taiwan-specific study of momentum net of the 0.3% sell tax and brokerage was found. Taiwan's cost structure (fixed tax, no stamp on buys) differs from the US spread-based estimates.
- No evidence found on optimal holding periods shorter than one month for momentum; the 2-to-4-week end of the user's range is outside what the cited literature tests, and short-horizon reversal (1 month) is a known opposing effect.

## 4. Market-timing overlays: moving-average / trend filters on the index

### Takeaway
A long moving-average filter on the index is best classified as **[DD-only]**: it reliably cuts volatility and deep drawdowns, but the claim that it raises return over buy-and-hold is contested and largely disappears in genuinely out-of-sample tests with costs. Long lookbacks (about 10 months / 200 days, evaluated monthly) are more robust than short ones because short lookbacks trade far more often and lose to whipsaw and costs. Taiwan evidence points the same way: the advantage of MA rules on 0050/TAIEX comes almost entirely from 2008, and the "best" parameter is unstable across sub-periods.

### Cited Findings
- Faber, "A Quantitative Approach to Tactical Asset Allocation" (working paper 2006; Journal of Wealth Management 2007; updates 2009, 2013): rule is long when month-end price is above the 10-month SMA, else cash; evaluated once a month. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/long-term-outperformance-from-trends-defined-by-moving-averages)
- Faber 2013 update: by gross Sharpe ratio, timing beats buy-and-hold robustly across 3-, 6-, 9- and 12-month SMAs; the model is at least 60% invested about 80% of the time, average exposure about 70%. CXO flags material data-snooping risk in the choice of 10 months for US stocks. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/long-term-outperformance-from-trends-defined-by-moving-averages)
- Secondary summary of Faber's US data, 1901–2008: about 14.42% annualised return with 14.30% volatility when above the 10-month SMA, versus 3.03% with 24.18% volatility when below. Secondary source, in-sample. — [Substack summary](https://onveston.substack.com/p/decades-of-dominance-the-investing)
- **[Contested]** Zakamulin, "Revisiting the Profitability of Market Timing with Moving Averages" (International Review of Finance 2018): the MA strategy's advantage over buy-and-hold is at most small and statistically indistinguishable from buy-and-hold; earlier "too good to be true" results (e.g. Glabadanidis 2015) stem from look-ahead bias. — [RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/irvfin/v18y2018i2p317-327.html)
- **[Contested]** Zakamulin (Journal of Asset Management 2014, "The real-life performance of market timing with moving average and time-series momentum rules"): OOS tests with realistic transaction costs show reported performance is highly overstated by data-mining. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/jam.2014.25)
- Zakamulin, 155-year US dataset: no statistically significant evidence that timing strategies outperformed the market in the second half of the sample; neither the shape of the MA weighting function nor the OOS estimation scheme improved rules. — [SSRN 2677212](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2677212)
- **[Contested]** Huang, Li, Wang & Zhou, "Time series momentum: Is it there?" (JFE 2020, 135(3), 774–794), challenging Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012): asset-by-asset regressions show little evidence of time-series momentum in- or out-of-sample; the large pooled t-statistic fails bootstrap critical values; the TSM strategy is profitable but performs virtually the same as a strategy based on the historical mean that needs no predictability. — [RePEc abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:135:y:2020:i:3:p:774-794)
- Clare et al. (2013), S&P 500: long-interval trend rules beat buy-and-hold; short-term signals give far worse returns because of over-trading; monthly evaluation beats daily. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/following-sp-500-index-trends)

**Taiwan-specific**
- NTU thesis on TAIEX technical rules: except for a "quarterly-line slope" rule and daily MACD, buy-and-hold of the TAIEX had the higher cumulative return than the technical rules tested; rules work in clear bull or bear phases and generate many flip-flop signals in range-bound markets. — [NTU thesis repository](https://tdr.lib.ntu.edu.tw/handle/123456789/70728)
- Independent 0050 analysis (iThome): 0050 had drawdowns over 15% ten times in 21 years and over 40% only in 2008; the MA rule's advantage comes almost entirely from 2008 and vanishes if that year is removed; the best MA length was about 90 days in the first half of the sample and 240 days in the second, i.e. unstable. Blog-level, but it reports its own negative result. — [iThome](https://ithelp.ithome.com.tw/articles/10410241)
- 0050 "wait for a dip" test over 23 years: across 196 variants of waiting for a 5–20% pullback before investing, average annualised return was below simply holding. Media report of a backtest. — [China Times Wantrich (one of two returned articles; confirm which)](https://wantrich.chinatimes.com/news/20260531900004-420101)

### Inferences
- For backtesting, treat the index filter as a risk switch, not an alpha source. Success criterion: max drawdown and worst-year loss fall materially while CAGR falls by less than a pre-set tolerance. Expect CAGR to fall in a market like Taiwan 2009–2026, where only one or two deep bear markets occurred.
- Why short lookbacks whipsaw: a 20-day MA is crossed many times a year by ordinary noise, each crossing costs a round trip plus the gap between the sell and re-buy price, and the average trend it captures is too short to pay for that. Long lookbacks trade a few times per decade-scale cycle. The cited studies (Clare et al.; Zakamulin) show the ranking empirically but do not quantify trades per year here.
- Robustness check implied by the iThome and Zakamulin results: require the filter to work across a plateau of lookbacks (e.g. 150–250 days) and across sub-periods excluding 2008; reject it if performance depends on one length or one crash.
- The weaker application is the more defensible one: use the index filter to block new entries (and tighten stops) rather than to force-sell existing strong positions. The momentum-state evidence in section 5 supports that use independently of whether MA timing beats buy-and-hold on the index itself.
- When the filter is off, the choice is cash versus 0050. The "wait for a dip" result argues against sitting in cash waiting for a better price; the filter should be a rule with a defined re-entry, not discretion.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of a 10-month / 200-day MA filter on TAIEX with CAGR, Sharpe and max drawdown versus buy-and-hold was retrieved. The Taiwan evidence is a thesis abstract and practitioner backtests.
- Faber's own post-publication (2007 onward) out-of-sample numbers were not retrieved.
- Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012) headline numbers were not retrieved; only the Huang et al. critique was.
- I found no quantitative source for trades-per-year and whipsaw loss by lookback length on TAIEX; this is straightforward to compute in the user's own backtest.

## 5. Momentum conditional on market state; volatility-managed momentum

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence in the whole topic is for conditioning stock momentum on market state: momentum profits in the US follow UP markets only, and in Taiwan the general absence of momentum has been attributed to the frequency of DOWN states, where momentum returns are more negative. Volatility scaling roughly doubles the Sharpe of long-short momentum in-sample, but the broader volatility-managed-portfolio result fails real-time and after-cost tests, and for a long-only retail trader it reduces to "hold less when recent volatility is high", which is implementable but is risk control, not a proven return enhancer.

### Cited Findings
- Cooper, Gutierrez & Hameed, "Market States and Momentum" (Journal of Finance 2004, 59(3), 1345–1365), US stocks: UP state = lagged 3-year market return non-negative, DOWN = negative. Momentum profits exclusively follow UP markets; after DOWN states there is no short-run momentum and significant long-run reversal. — [Paper copy](https://typeset.io/pdf/market-states-and-momentum-4wtc7g4rqv.pdf)
- Du, Huang & Liao, "Why is there no momentum in the Taiwan stock market?" (Journal of Economics and Business 2009, 61(2), 140–152): DOWN markets occur more often in Taiwan than in the US and momentum profits are more negative after DOWN markets in Taiwan; they attribute the lack of momentum profits to this state dependence rather than to culture. — [RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jebusi/v61y2009i2p140-152.html); [NAU record](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/why-is-there-no-momentum-in-the-taiwan-stock-market/)
- Chui, Titman & Wei, "Individualism and Momentum around the World" (Journal of Finance 2010, 65(1), 361–392): momentum profits are positively related to a country's individualism score; East Asian markets show weak momentum. A working paper citing it states Taiwan (Hofstede individualism 17) has insignificant momentum profits versus significant profits in the US (score 91). — [RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v65y2010i1p361-392.html); [Durham working paper](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/preview/1418688/13682.pdf)
- An older Taiwan study of listed companies reports return reversal rather than momentum for some formation/holding combinations. — [Thesis record](https://openaccess.library.uitm.edu.my/Record/ndltd-TW-096KUAS0213045/Details)
- Daniel & Moskowitz, "Momentum Crashes" (NBER WP 20439, 2014; JFE 2016): crashes occur in "panic" states, after market declines when volatility is high, and coincide with sharp market rebounds. In bear markets the momentum portfolio's up-market beta is −1.51 versus down-market beta −0.70 (t-stat of difference 4.5), behaving like a written call on the market; most of the asymmetry comes from the past-loser (short) leg. A dynamic strategy based on forecasts of momentum's mean and variance approximately doubles alpha and Sharpe. — [NBER w20439](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439)
- Barroso & Santa-Clara, "Momentum has its moments" (JFE 2015, 116(1), 111–120): scaling long-short momentum by its realised volatility (6-month window of daily returns) nearly doubles Sharpe, from 0.53 to 0.97, and virtually eliminates crashes; the largest gains are in the higher moments. — [RePEc abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:116:y:2015:i:1:p:111-120); [ETF.com summary](https://www.etf.com/node/94292.md)
- Barroso (CFA Institute blog, 2018): momentum's Sharpe ratio is about 1.7 after "safe" (low-volatility) months and 0.28 after "risky" months. — [CFA Institute blog](https://blogs.cfainstitute.org/blog/2018/01/17/timing-the-market-momentum-and-beyond)
- **[Contested]** Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang & Yan, "On the performance of volatility-managed portfolios" (JFE 2020, 138(1), 95–117), 103 equity strategies: the in-sample spanning-regression alphas of Moreira & Muir (2017) are confirmed, but volatility-managed portfolios do not systematically beat unmanaged ones in direct comparison; real-time versions generally earn lower certainty-equivalent returns and Sharpe ratios, because of structural instability in the spanning regressions. — [RePEc abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:138:y:2020:i:1:p:95-117); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/does-portfolio-timing-based-on-volatility-signals-outperform-buy-and-hold/)
- **[Contested]** Barroso & Detzel (2021), as summarised in a thesis: after transaction costs none of the volatility-managed portfolios generate positive alpha and most have significantly lower Sharpe than unmanaged versions. Secondary citation. — [Erasmus thesis](https://thesis.eur.nl/pub/72898/thesis_ggianfranceschi_third_draft.pdf)

### Inferences
- Concrete, backtestable state filter for Taiwan: allow new momentum entries only when the market is in an UP state. Cooper et al. use the 3-year market return; a shorter definition (12-month TAIEX return positive, or TAIEX above its 200-day MA) is a practical proxy but is not what was tested and should be compared against the 36-month version.
- Daniel–Moskowitz crash risk is mostly a short-leg phenomenon, so a long-only winner portfolio does not suffer the classic crash. The long-only cost in a rebound is different: past winners are low-beta/defensive at the bottom and lag the index, so a long-only momentum trader underperforms 0050 in the first leg of a recovery. This argues for holding 0050 (not high-momentum stocks, and not cash) immediately after the market turns up from a bear state.
- Long-only volatility management without leverage or derivatives means scaling exposure between 0% and 100%: e.g. target position size inversely proportional to the stock's or the market's recent realised volatility, capped at 100%. That is implementable at end of day with a spreadsheet, but because exposure cannot exceed 100% it can only remove return in calm periods' absence of leverage; expect **[DD-only]**.
- Given Cederburg et al. and Barroso–Detzel, do not expect volatility scaling to add return net of 0.6–0.9% costs; if used, apply it at entry (position sizing) rather than by continuously re-trading existing positions.
- The Taiwan "no momentum" literature (samples mostly before about 2008) is a caution: unconditional price momentum is not a reliable edge in Taiwan, and any rule set should be validated on post-2010 data and be conditional on market state. The 52-week-high variant has the better Taiwan evidence (section 3).

### Gaps
- Cooper et al.'s exact monthly profit figures (from memory about +0.93% per month after UP states and −0.37% after DOWN states for 6-month momentum, 1929–1995) could not be verified against a source in this session; confirm before quoting.
- Barroso & Santa-Clara's skewness, kurtosis and worst-month figures, and Moreira & Muir's headline numbers, were not retrieved.
- No study of volatility-managed long-only momentum in Taiwan was found, and no post-2015 Taiwan momentum study with updated market-state results.
- The exact beta figures for the long-only winner decile in rebounds were not retrieved; the statement that winners lag in rebounds is inferred from the beta asymmetry.

## 6. Re-entry after a market crash: bottoming signals versus trend re-entry

### Takeaway
I found no rigorous, out-of-sample evidence that bottoming signals (breadth thrusts, VIX peaks, credit spreads, foreign-flow reversals) reliably time re-entry; what exists is practitioner commentary on very small samples with acknowledged false signals. The best and worst days cluster together near bottoms, so exiting costs the rebound unless there is a mechanical re-entry rule, and price-based trend re-entry is the only approach with a long documented record, at the price of re-entering late.

### Cited Findings
- Wells Fargo Investment Institute, S&P 500 1994–2024: missing the 30 best days cut average annual return from 8.0% to 1.8%, below the 2.5% inflation rate of the period. — [Wells Fargo](https://www.wellsfargo.com/investment-institute/sr-perils-time-volatile-markets)
- Fidelity data, 1988–2023: USD 10,000 grew to USD 417,995 fully invested, versus about USD 264,000 if the five best days were missed. — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/5-day-trap-missing-less-153408337.html)
- Since 1988 a best or worst day occurred within 21 trading days of another best or worst day more than 70% of the time. — [Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/is-buy-and-hold-really-the-best-investing-strategy)
- Morningstar's check of the "missing the best days" argument: the benefit of avoiding the worst days is about as large as the penalty of missing the best days, but because the two cluster together, avoiding only the worst is practically impossible. So the statistic is an argument against discretionary in-and-out, not proof that being out during high-volatility regimes is costly. — [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/funds/you-can-beat-stock-market-by-avoiding-its-worst-days-you-wont)
- Zweig Breadth Thrust (10-day EMA of NYSE advances/(advances+declines) moving from below 0.40 to above 0.615 within 10 trading days): a SentimenTrader review over about 60 years found mid- to long-term returns above random but "too many false signals for comfort", and acknowledges the sample size is too low. Vendor source. — [SentimenTrader](https://sentimentrader.com/blog/a-failed-zweig-breadth-thrust-does-it-matter)
- Sceptical view: the ZBT is a data-mined signal with as many false positives as true positives that missed most bear-market bottoms. Opinion piece, not a formal test. — [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4778098-bull-market-indicated-by-zweig-breadth-thrust-signal-trust-it)
- Daniel & Moskowitz: the transition out of a bear market is a sharp rebound concentrated in high-volatility "panic" states, which is when momentum portfolios do worst. — [NBER w20439](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439)
- Faber-type rule: re-entry is simply the month-end close back above the 10-month SMA, evaluated monthly. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/long-term-outperformance-from-trends-defined-by-moving-averages)

### Inferences
- Trend re-entry is "robust" only in the sense that it is mechanical, cannot stay out forever, and has no parameters fitted to a handful of historical bottoms. It guarantees missing the first leg of a V-shaped recovery; that lag is the main reason MA timing fails to beat buy-and-hold in the Zakamulin tests.
- Any exit rule must be specified together with its re-entry rule and backtested as a pair. The observable cost of a market-exit rule is (re-entry price minus exit price) plus two round trips, per cycle.
- Bottoming signals have at most a few dozen independent observations (bear-market lows), so they cannot be validated statistically. If used at all, use them only to permit a partial 0050 position earlier than the trend rule, never as the sole trigger.
- A two-stage re-entry fits the evidence in sections 5 and 6: stage 1, when the index trend filter turns positive, hold 0050 (captures beta in the rebound, when momentum stocks lag); stage 2, resume individual momentum entries once the market is in an UP state by the longer definition and new 52-week highs reappear.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of VIX-peak, credit-spread or breadth-thrust re-entry signals was found; "no reliable evidence found" is itself the finding, but it reflects limited search depth as well as a thin literature.
- No Taiwan-specific evidence was found on foreign institutional investor (外資) net-buying reversals as a re-entry signal, or on the cost of missing the rebound on TAIEX (e.g. 2009, 2020, 2022–23, 2025).
- No quantified estimate of average re-entry lag (percentage above the low at which a 200-day rule re-enters) was retrieved for TAIEX or the S&P 500; compute it in backtest.

## 7. Taiwan-specific market microstructure that affects exits

### Takeaway
A stop price in Taiwan is not an executable price: daily price limits (±10% per the brief), the documented magnet effect near limits, and restricted matching for disposition stocks all mean a breakout stock can gap or lock limit-down through a stop, so realised loss on the worst trades will exceed the nominal stop width. The mitigation that follows from the evidence is position sizing for a multi-limit-down loss, avoiding or pre-emptively handling disposition-prone names, and executing end-of-day decisions the next session rather than relying on resting stop orders.

### Cited Findings
- Cho, Russell, Tiao & Tsay (Journal of Empirical Finance 2003), TSE intraday data: prices accelerate toward the upper limit, with weaker evidence of acceleration toward the lower limit; the effect survives controls for momentum. First empirical verification of the magnet effect. — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:empfin:v:10:y:2003:i:1-2:p:133-168)
- Hsieh, Kim & Yang (Journal of Empirical Finance 2009), TSE transaction data, logit model: the probability of a price increase (decrease) rises as price approaches the upper (lower) limit; information-based trading affects the lower-limit effect. — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:empfin:v:16:y:2009:i:5:p:830-837)
- Wong, Chang & Tu (Pacific-Basin Finance Journal 2009): significant magnet effects on the TSE, driven by uninformed individual traders; no significant effect when institutions dominate volume. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/pacfin/v17y2009i1p28-40.html)
- Chang & Chang (International Journal of Business and Finance Research 2021): after Taiwan relaxed its daily price limits the magnet effect largely disappeared, especially on the downside, and volatility fell. — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ibf:ijbfre:v:15:y:2021:i:1:p:33-44)
- Disposition stocks (處置股) reform effective 10 Aug 2026: disposition period shortened to 5 business days for first and repeat dispositions (previously 10), or 7 business days (previously 12) when the stock is also flagged for a high day-trading share; call-auction matching unified at about every 2 minutes (previously every 5 or 20 minutes). — [UDN](https://udn.com/news/story/7251/9668143); [UDN](https://udn.com/news/story/7251/9668728)
- Pre-funding (預收款券) still applies to disposition stocks after the reform; the exchange acknowledges it reduces trading volume. — [UDN Money](https://money.udn.com/money/amp/story/5607/9775106); [UAnalyze](https://uanalyze.com.tw/articles/3908252721)
- Earlier rule (2021): a disposed stock whose day-trading share exceeded 60% had its disposition extended by 2 days. Superseded in part by the 2026 reform. — [CNA](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/afe/202108010192.aspx)
- Day-trading tax: the securities transaction tax on same-day offsetting cash trades is 0.15% (halved from 0.3%), first effective 28 Apr 2017 and extended, by an amendment passed on 31 Dec 2024, through 31 Dec 2027. — [TechNews](https://finance.technews.tw/2024/08/08/day-trading-tax/); [Commercial Times](https://www.ctee.com.tw/news/20250101700052-430104)
- Securities transaction tax is assessed by settlement date, not trade date. — [UDN Money](https://money.udn.com/money/story/5613/9388560)
- Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean (2007): Taiwan individual investors are about twice as likely to sell winners as losers. — [RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/eufman/v13y2007i3p423-447.html)
- Taiwan is a market in which more than 70% of investors are individuals, per a Taiwan 52-week-high / herding study. — [vLex record](https://international.vlex.com/vid/the-analysis-of-52week-855636556)

### Inferences
- Worst-case sizing: with a ±10% limit, a stop set 10% below entry can be skipped by a single limit-down open, and two consecutive locked limit-down days produce about −19% before any fill. Size each position so that a 2-to-3-limit-down loss, not the nominal stop, is the tolerable portfolio hit.
- The magnet evidence suggests that once a stock is within a few percent of limit-down intraday, the probability of reaching the limit rises, which favours selling at the next available price over waiting for a bounce; but the effect is weaker on the downside and reportedly faded after limits were widened, so do not build an intraday rule on it.
- Breakout/momentum scanners select exactly the stocks that trigger attention and disposition measures (large multi-day gains, high turnover, high day-trading share). During disposition, call-auction matching and pre-funding cut liquidity, so exits are slower and at worse prices. Backtestable rules: (a) exclude stocks currently under disposition from new entries; (b) flag held stocks approaching disposition criteria and test whether exiting before disposition beats holding through it. The 2026 reform (5 days, 2-minute matching) reduces but does not remove this friction, and pre-2026 backtests overstate it relative to current rules.
- The 0.15% day-trade tax rate applies only to same-day round trips; a 2-week-to-3-month holder pays the full 0.3% on sells, consistent with the brief's 0.6–0.9% round-trip assumption. Whether the 0.15% rate survives beyond 2027 is undecided.
- T+2 settlement means sale proceeds can fund a same-day or next-day purchase under normal netting, but for disposition stocks pre-funding breaks that: cash and shares must be in place before the order.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a TWSE primary rule page confirming the ±10% daily limit (and its June 2015 change from ±7%), the 0.3% securities transaction tax, the 0.1425% brokerage cap, or T+2 settlement. These are taken from the research brief and general knowledge; confirm on twse.com.tw.
- No study was found quantifying how often stops fail to execute because of limit-down locks in Taiwan, or the average slippage beyond a stop on such days. This is computable from daily data (count closes at limit-down with thin volume following a stop trigger).
- No evidence was found on ex-dividend season effects (除權息, mostly June–September) on momentum or stop signals. Practical point without a source: backtests must use dividend-adjusted prices or ex-dividend gaps will trigger false stops and false moving-average breaks.
- No study was found on returns of stocks entering or leaving disposition status under the post-August-2026 rules; the reform is too recent.
- The 2026 disposition reform details come from news reports, not from the TWSE announcement itself.
